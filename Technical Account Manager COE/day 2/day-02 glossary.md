## Exercises

### Exercise 1 — Glossary: 20 terms in client language
How we build, how we ship, how we measure, and how we plan.

| # | Term | In plain words | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Extreme Programming (XP) | A way of working built on short cycles, constant feedback, and a few habits done every day: writing tests first, working in pairs, small releases, and steady tidying. | Changes are cheap to make late in the project, so you can change your mind without paying a big penalty. |
| 2 | Test-Driven Development (TDD) | Before writing a feature, we write a small automatic check that says what "working" looks like. Then we build until the check passes. | Fewer bugs reach your users, and every feature comes with proof that it works. |
| 3 | Pair programming | Two engineers work on the same task at one screen. One types, the other reviews and thinks ahead, and they swap often. | Mistakes are caught as they happen, and knowledge never sits with one person who could leave or fall ill. |
| 4 | Refactoring | Tidying the inside of the software without changing what it does, like reorganizing a kitchen without changing the menu. | The product stays easy and cheap to change in year three, not just in month one. |
| 5 | Automated tests | Checks the computer runs by itself, in minutes, every time anything changes. | Every release is checked the same thorough way, without relying on someone remembering to check. |
| 6 | Technical debt | Shortcuts taken today that make future changes slower, like interest on a loan. | Some is fine on purpose; unmanaged debt is why features slow down over time. We tell you when we take it on. |
| 7 | API | A menu one piece of software offers to another: "you can ask me for these things, in this format". A payment API lets your app take card payments without building a bank. | Your systems can connect to partners, apps and tools without rebuilding them. |
| 8 | Version control | A complete, dated history of every change to the code and who made it. | Nothing is ever lost, and we can undo any change quickly. |
| 9 | Continuous Integration (CI) | Everyone's work is combined and checked several times a day, instead of in one big merge at the end. | Problems show up within the hour they were made, while they are still cheap to fix. |
| 10 | Continuous Delivery (CD) | The product is always in a releasable state. Releasing is a business decision, not a technical project. | You choose when to go live, and a release is a non-event instead of a weekend of risk. |
| 11 | Continuous Deployment | One step further: every change that passes all checks goes live automatically. | Improvements reach customers in hours, not quarters. |
| 12 | Deployment pipeline | The automated assembly line that builds, checks and releases the software. | Releases are repeatable and fast, with no manual steps to get wrong. |
| 13 | Small releases | Shipping a little at a time, often, rather than everything at once. | You see real progress every week and can steer based on real user feedback. |
| 14 | Feature flag | An on/off switch for a feature that is already released but hidden. | You can launch to 5% of users first, or switch something off in seconds without a new release. |
| 15 | User story | One small piece of value, written from the user's view: "As a shopper, I want to save my cart so I can buy later." | Work is planned and prioritised in terms you care about, not technical tasks. |
| 16 | Spec-driven development | Writing down clearly what we will build, and how we will know it is right, before building it. With AI, the written spec is what guides the AI. | Fewer misunderstandings, and AI speed without AI guesswork. |
| 17 | Deployment frequency | How often we release changes to your users. | A sign of how quickly your ideas turn into something customers can use. |
| 18 | Lead time for changes | Time from a change being finished by an engineer to it being live for users. | How long you wait for a fix or idea to reach customers. |
| 19 | Change failure rate | Out of all releases, the share that causes a problem and needs a fix. | How safe our speed is. Fast and careless does not count. |
